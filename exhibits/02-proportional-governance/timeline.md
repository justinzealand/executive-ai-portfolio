## What the arc shows

The control outran the operator. A review gate that was mechanically sound produced a queue growing at ~1.8 plans/day net, and once its nag rate crossed the operator's capacity the signal degraded into noise — the 07-18 investigation found the real generator was a template defect, not reviewer laziness, but by then the operator's lived experience was "plan review is defective," and one sentence in a chat session retired the gate. That reactive retirement then hardened into durable policy (v10h) because nothing in the system compared the decision against a goals layer: the charter recorded *that* the operator had authorized retirement, not *why*, and the kernel's own acceptance metrics — which contradicted the premise four days later — had no consumer that could reopen the question. The restoration was proportional rather than a reversal: the three-tier policy the retirement had produced was kept, the engine was re-attached only to the two tiers where review earns its cost, the plan restoring it was reviewed by the thing it restored, and the success signals gained stable ids so that future plans cite the goals they advance mechanically — the missing layer that would have caught the drift the first time.
